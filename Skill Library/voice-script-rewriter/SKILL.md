# Voice Script Rewriter

Transform written scripts into natural, conversational content that sounds like you when spoken aloud—with key phrases bolded for easy visual scanning during delivery.

## How This Works

1. **Analyze the script** — Identify core message, key points, and current tone
2. **Learn your voice** — Ask clarifying questions to understand your natural speaking patterns
3. **Rewrite conversationally** — Transform formal text into how you'd actually say it
4. **Bold strategically** — Highlight scannable anchor phrases for confident delivery

## Core Rewriting Principles

### Write Like You Speak

Conversational speech differs fundamentally from written prose. Natural speaking includes:

- **Contractions** — "you're" not "you are", "I'll" not "I will"
- **Shorter sentences** — 12-15 words max, matching natural breath patterns
- **Sentence fragments** — Sometimes. Like this. Because that's how we talk.
- **Active voice** — "I built this" beats "This was built by me"
- **Direct address** — "you" instead of "one" or "people"
- **Casual connectors** — Start sentences with "And", "But", "So" when it flows naturally

### Preserve Meaning, Change Delivery

The rewrite keeps your core message intact while shifting how it's expressed:

- Formal: "It is imperative that organizations prioritize..."
- Conversational: "Look, companies need to focus on..."

Both say the same thing. One sounds like a person talking.

### Strategic Bolding for Scanning

Bold formatting serves two purposes:

1. **Visual anchors** — Your eyes catch key phrases instantly when glancing down
2. **Emphasis cues** — Reminds you which words carry weight

Bold 3-5 word phrases that capture core ideas, not individual words. Bold the concept, not just a keyword.

**Good bolding:** "The biggest challenge we face is **getting teams to actually use** the tools we build."

**Poor bolding:** "The biggest **challenge** we face is getting **teams** to actually **use** the tools we **build**."

Aim for one bolded phrase every 2-3 sentences—enough to guide your eye without visual clutter.

## Understanding Voice Patterns

Before rewriting, gather context about the speaker's natural style:

**Essential questions:**
- What's the setting? (Interview, presentation, podcast, video, internal meeting)
- Who's the audience? (Industry experts, general public, team members, customers)
- What's your natural speaking style? (Energetic, measured, technical, storytelling-focused)
- Any specific phrases or words you use often?
- Topics or jargon to avoid?

**Voice calibration examples:**

*Energetic speaker:* Short punchy sentences. Lots of emphasis. Questions to the audience. "Here's the thing—" as a transition.

*Measured expert:* Slightly longer sentences with clear structure. "What we're seeing is..." "The key insight here..." Pauses for effect.

*Storyteller:* Narrative flow. "So here's what happened..." Personal anecdotes. Builds to a point rather than stating it upfront.

## Rewriting Patterns

### Remove Formality

**Before:** "We have observed a significant increase in user engagement metrics following the implementation of the new interface design."

**After:** "So we launched the new design, and **user engagement jumped significantly**. People are actually sticking around."

### Add Natural Pauses

Use punctuation to create breathing room:

- Em dashes for asides — like this one — that add context
- Ellipses for trailing thoughts or dramatic pauses...
- Short sentences. That stand alone. For emphasis.

### Make It Scannable

When someone's reading aloud, they glance down, grab a phrase, look up, and speak. Structure supports this:

**Dense paragraph:**
"The challenge we face in modern software development is that teams often struggle to balance velocity with quality, leading to technical debt accumulation that eventually slows future development, creating a vicious cycle that's hard to escape without dedicated refactoring time."

**Scannable version:**
Here's the challenge: **teams want to move fast**, but they also need quality.

When you rush, **technical debt piles up**. And that debt? It slows you down later.

You end up in this vicious cycle. The only way out is **dedicated time to clean things up**.

### Conversational Connectors

How real people transition between ideas:

- "Here's the thing..."
- "So what does this mean?"
- "Now, you might be thinking..."
- "Let me give you an example..."
- "The reality is..."
- "And look—"

## Common Pitfalls

**Too casual** — Slang and overly informal language can undermine credibility. Match the formality level to the context. A keynote needs more polish than a team standup.

**Over-bolding** — If everything's bold, nothing stands out. Aim for 20-30% of text bolded at most.

**Losing the message** — Conversational doesn't mean vague. Every sentence should still serve the core point.

**Ignoring rhythm** — Read it aloud. If you run out of breath mid-sentence, it's too long. If it sounds choppy, combine some thoughts.

## Delivery Tips (Include When Relevant)

After rewriting, remind the speaker:

- **Practice aloud** — The script should feel natural in your mouth, not just on the page
- **Use bold as glance points** — Look down, grab the bolded phrase, look up, expand on it naturally
- **Don't memorize word-for-word** — Treat it as talking points, not a teleprompter
- **Mark your pauses** — Add "/" or "..." where you want to breathe or let a point land

## Example Transformation

### Original Script

"In today's rapidly evolving technological landscape, organizations must prioritize adaptability and innovation to remain competitive. The traditional approaches to software development are no longer sufficient to meet the demands of modern users who expect seamless experiences across multiple platforms and devices."

### Rewritten for Natural Delivery

Look, **technology's moving faster than ever**. And if your company isn't adapting? You're going to fall behind.

The old ways of building software just don't cut it anymore. Users expect **seamless experiences everywhere**—on their phone, their laptop, their tablet. They don't care about your technical constraints.

### Analysis

- Formal "In today's rapidly evolving" → Conversational "Look, technology's moving faster than ever"
- Removed jargon ("technological landscape")
- Split one long sentence into three shorter, punchier ones
- Added direct address ("your company", "You're")
- Bolded core concepts for scanning
- Ended with user perspective for relatability

## Workflow

1. **Read the original script** — Understand the full message and structure
2. **Ask clarifying questions** — Context, audience, speaker's natural style
3. **Rewrite section by section** — Transform 2-3 paragraphs at a time, maintaining flow
4. **Add bold formatting** — Highlight key scannable phrases
5. **Read aloud mentally** — Does it sound like a human talking?
6. **Refine** — Adjust rhythm, emphasis, and bolding based on how it flows

The goal: When the speaker glances at the page, they see their next thought clearly bolded. When they read it aloud, it sounds like them—not like they're reading someone else's words.
