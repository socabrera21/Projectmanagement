## 1. Company Deep Dive

### Business Model & Value Proposition

AirGarage is a full-stack parking operating system for commercial real estate owners. Founded in 2017 by three ASU students, the company began as a peer-to-peer driveway marketplace and has evolved into the operating layer for institutional-grade parking assets. Their model: property owners hand over operational control, AirGarage handles everything (payments, dynamic pricing, enforcement, customer service), and owners receive roughly 70% of net revenue — far more than they'd earn under traditional ground-lease agreements with legacy operators.

The business is SaaS-adjacent in behavior but operates on a revenue-share model. This is key for your positioning: you're selling a platform that generates ongoing, recurring economics for property owners — not a one-time product. That framing (platform, recurring value, measurable NOI improvement) maps directly to your enterprise SaaS background.

## 4. Anticipated Questions & Suggested Answers

### Background & Experience

**Q: Walk me through your background.**
I've spent the last several years selling enterprise AI and SaaS platforms to Fortune 1000 companies — Rapid7 in security, Jamf for device management, Bonterra, Agiloft for CLM, and most recently Aisera for 4+ years. The common thread is full-cycle, complex sales: I identify the business problem, map the stakeholders, run the evaluation, and close the deal. I've consistently hit 110-120% of quota and closed $1M+ ARR deals involving IT, Legal, Finance, and C-suite. I'm at a point where I want to apply that discipline in a company at the growth inflection point — which is exactly what drew me to AirGarage.
- *Metrics:* 110-120% quota attainment, $1M+ ARR, 4+ years at Aisera

**Q: Why AirGarage?**
A few things converged. First, the fundamentals: 234% NRR at 24 months, cash flow positive, 10x revenue growth since Series A. That tells me the product creates real, compounding value. Second, the market timing: parking is a $3.3B+ market with massive fragmentation and a legacy operator problem. AirGarage is building the infrastructure layer. And third, the JD's honesty about the leadership path — you're not just hiring an AE to hit quota, you're building the future management layer.

**Q: Tell me about a deal you're most proud of.**
At Aisera, I was assigned a Fortune 500 financial services firm that had just had a failed AI implementation. I found the VP of IT Service Delivery as my champion — she needed a win after putting her credibility on the line. I ran a tightly scoped 90-day POC, documented 38% ticket deflection weekly, which gave her the ammunition to re-engage the CFO. Closed at $1.2M ARR in a 9-month cycle, expanded 40% in year two.
- *Metrics:* $1.2M ARR, 9-month cycle, 40% expansion, 38% POC deflection rate

**Q: How do you approach prospecting in a new territory or vertical?**
Research-heavy first two weeks: understand who the highest-value targets are and what trigger events create urgency. For AirGarage that means reading CRE portfolios — who owns significant parking assets, which markets are underserved, what operational signals suggest readiness (expiring operator contracts, new acquisitions). Top 20 accounts get highly personalized outreach with a specific hypothesis about their parking revenue gap. Cold calls as primary channel — they surface objections fast and qualify in real time.

### Behavioral Questions

**Q: Tell me about a time you lost a deal and what you learned.**
Lost an $800K deal at Aisera to a do-nothing decision. My champion couldn't make the ROI case stick internally without me in the room — I hadn't equipped her well enough. What I changed: a formal 'champion readiness check' before any deal enters late stage. I ask my champion to walk me through how they'd present the business case if I wasn't there. If they can't, I know I have more work to do. My late-stage loss rate dropped significantly.

**Q: How do you handle a quarter where you're behind on quota?**
Diagnose first. Deal-by-deal audit: which have genuine momentum, which am I being optimistic about without evidence. Close-plan aggressively on the real ones, stop over-investing in false pipeline. Accelerate prospecting to fill the back half of next quarter — you can't solve a pipeline problem in the same quarter. Involve the manager early — not to signal distress, but because a second set of eyes on deal strategy is valuable.

**Q: Give me an example of selling to a completely new buyer type.**
At Bonterra I moved from enterprise tech buyers to nonprofit leadership. Had to translate value into their language: volunteer hours saved, donor retention rates, program outcomes. 60 days of active learning — reading sector publications, shadowing CSMs. By Q3 I was a top performer. At AirGarage I'll study the CRE buyer: what asset managers care about (NOI, occupancy, liability), and position in their terms.

### Role-Specific Questions

**Q: How would you build your pipeline in a market you've never sold into?**
Start with the tools in your stack — Reonomy and Crexi for property ownership visibility, Zoominfo for decision-makers. Segment by asset type and portfolio size: institutional owners managing 10+ parking assets are highest-value — revenue impact is large enough to move fast. Look for trigger events: new acquisitions, expiring operator agreements, recent property sales. Mine customer language through existing AirGarage customers — that becomes the foundation of every cold outreach message.

**Q: What do you know about how AirGarage makes money?**
Revenue-share model: ~30% to AirGarage, 70% to property owner. Compelling story vs. a flat ground-lease fee regardless of revenue generated. The 234% NRR means customers don't just stay — they expand, probably by adding locations or services. For a salesperson: every deal I close becomes more valuable over time. Pipeline compounds.
