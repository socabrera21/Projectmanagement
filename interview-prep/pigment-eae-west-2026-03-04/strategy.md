## 2. The Narrative Arc

Each round has a different audience with a different lens. Shift emphasis accordingly — never give the same pitch twice.

### Round 1 — Recruiter Screen (30 min, phone)
**Their job:** Qualify you against the job description checkbox list.
**Your job:** Clear the filter, generate curiosity, earn Round 2.
**Emphasis:** Quota attainment numbers, enterprise deal size, full-cycle experience, and one crisp gap-bridge on FP&A. Do not over-explain. Leave them wanting to ask follow-up questions.

### Round 2 — Hiring Manager
**Their job:** Determine if you can sell to their ICP in their motion.
**Your job:** Show you understand Pigment's buyer (CFO/VP Finance), their pain (manual planning, Excel hell, Anaplan rigidity), and that you've navigated equivalent complexity.
**Emphasis:** Agiloft ROI models and business case construction. Aisera deal rescue and greenfield build. MEDDPICC rigor with multi-threaded executive access. Frame every story around the customer's transformation, not your number.

### Round 3 — Theater Leader (Culture/Collaboration)
**Their job:** Decide if you fit how Pigment teams operate.
**Your job:** Demonstrate intellectual humility, coachability, collaborative instinct, and the ability to ramp fast.
**Emphasis:** Jamf zero-churn streak (longevity = trust-building). Aisera territory build (resourcefulness without a playbook). Any story where you learned something mid-deal and course-corrected. Lean into curiosity and relationship-first language.

### Round 4 — Case Study / Discovery Call Simulation
**Their job:** See you run a real Pigment sales motion.
**Your job:** Run a textbook discovery call against a VP Finance persona — surface the pain of their current planning process, tie it to business impact, establish urgency, and move toward a next step.
**Emphasis:** Ask more than you tell. Use MEDDPICC to structure internally. Show comfort discussing CFO-level priorities (budget accuracy, headcount planning, scenario modeling). See Section 6 below for full case study prep.

### Round 5 — Global Leader
**Their job:** Evaluate long-term upside and executive presence.
**Your job:** Demonstrate growth orientation, self-awareness about your gaps, and a clear story of how you accelerate in new categories.
**Emphasis:** Your ramp track record (Aisera: new territory, above quota within quota period). Your pattern of learning product-new verticals quickly. Pigment as a career bet you've done the research to make intentionally, not a fallback.

### Round 6 — Founder
**Their job:** Make sure you believe in what they're building.
**Your job:** Show mission alignment without sycophancy. Have a genuine point of view on why Pigment matters in the market.
**Emphasis:** The shift from static planning to dynamic, collaborative planning is not a feature — it is a new category. Lean into your belief that AI-native planning tools will replace incumbent complexity-as-a-moat. Connect your personal experience watching organizations make decisions from outdated data.

## 6. Round-by-Round Strategy

### Round 1: Recruiter Screen

**Goal:** Clear the filter, earn Round 2.

**What to hit in the first 2 minutes:** Years of experience (8+), enterprise deal size ($150K–$500K ACV), full-cycle ownership, current quota attainment (103% at Agiloft, 111% at Aisera).

**What to volunteer early:** One sentence on the FP&A gap and one sentence bridging it. Don't wait for them to ask. "I haven't sold into FP&A specifically, but I've built ROI models for CFO stakeholders throughout my career and I've studied the Pigment competitive landscape — I'm ready to talk about that."

**What to hold back:** Deep deal stories, competitive opinions, gap details. Save depth for the HM.

**Closing move:** "I'd love to understand what the most important quality is for the person who succeeds in this role. Is it more about territory build or expansion of existing accounts?"

---

### Round 2: Hiring Manager

**What they'll probe:**
- How do you run discovery with a CFO?
- What's your biggest deal and why did you win?
- Walk me through a deal you almost lost.
- How do you build pipeline in a new territory?
- What do you know about our competitive positioning?

**How to answer:**
Every story should use the STAR structure but end on the customer's outcome, not your quota. "The customer reduced their planning cycle from 3 weeks to 4 days" lands better than "I hit 111% of quota."

**Stories to use:** Aisera deal rescue ($400K), Agiloft ROI model win, Jamf territory expansion. See Power Stories Matrix (Section 7).

**Competitive readiness:** Be ready to compare Pigment to Anaplan on three dimensions — flexibility, speed-to-value, and AI capability. See Competitor Matrix (Section 8).

**Closing move:** "What does success look like in the first 90 days for this role? I want to make sure I'm thinking about ramp the same way you are."

---

### Round 3: Theater Leader (Culture/Collaboration)

**Their lens:** Do you make your team better? Are you coachable? Will you be a cultural fit in a fast-moving, startup-adjacent environment?

**What they'll probe:**
- Tell me about a time you had to work across teams to close a deal.
- Describe a situation where you got critical feedback and how you handled it.
- What does your relationship with your SE/solutions team look like?

**Stories to use:**
- Jamf zero-churn streak: relationship-first selling, multi-year retention built on genuine partnership, not just renewal pressure.
- Aisera territory build: working without a playbook, leaning on internal resources creatively.
- Any moment where you course-corrected mid-deal based on customer feedback.

**Tone:** Curious, collaborative, genuinely interested in how Pigment's team operates. Ask questions about how the sales org works with the CS and product teams. Show you've thought about the company, not just the job.

**Closing move:** "What's one thing that separates the AEs who thrive here from the ones who struggle?"

---

### Round 4: Case Study / Discovery Call Simulation

**Likely format:** You are given a persona (VP Finance or CFO at a 10,000-person company) and asked to run a 30-minute discovery call. An interviewer plays the buyer.

**Structure:**

*Opening (2 min):* Set the agenda, establish that this is a conversation, not a pitch.
"I want to spend most of our time understanding your world — how you're running planning today, where the friction is. I'll share context on Pigment where it's relevant. Fair?"

*Discovery (15–18 min):* Go wide before you go deep. Sample questions:
- "Walk me through how your annual planning process works today — from kickoff to final sign-off."
- "Where does the process break down? What's the last thing to close and why?"
- "If a board member asked for a new scenario today, what would it take to get them an answer?"
- "What does version control look like across finance and the business units?"
- "If you could change one thing about how your team plans, what would it be?"

*Tie to impact (5 min):* Reflect back what you heard. Quantify the pain.
"So if I heard you right, your team is spending about 40% of the planning cycle on data reconciliation — that's weeks of effort that doesn't generate any insight. What does that mean in terms of decisions that get made late or not at all?"

*Next step (3 min):* Propose a concrete next step, not a generic follow-up.
"Based on what you've shared, it sounds like the right next conversation is a working session where I show you exactly how a company with your structure handles scenario modeling in Pigment — with your own planning structure mapped in. Does that make sense as a next step?"

**Red flags to avoid in the case study:** Pitching too early, talking about features before pain, not asking about current-state process, using jargon the buyer wouldn't use, closing without proposing a clear next step.

---

### Round 5: Global Leader

**Their lens:** Do you have a growth mindset? Can you operate at scale? Are you a long-term investment?

**What they'll probe:**
- What's the hardest thing you've had to learn in this job?
- Where have you grown the most in the last two years?
- Tell me about a failure and what you took from it.

**Stories to use:**
- Aisera greenfield: "I had to build the ICP definition from scratch. There was no playbook. I over-indexed on company size early and missed that the right trigger was a specific IT org structure. I recalibrated after quarter one and adjusted the territory plan."
- Learning Agiloft's CLM product: how you ramped on a technical product quickly.
- Any story about seeking coaching, feedback, or deliberate skill development.

**Tone:** Self-aware. You are not defensive about your gaps. You have a point of view on where you are in your development and what you want to learn next. Frame Pigment as a deliberate career bet.

**Closing move:** "What is the characteristic you see most often in the AEs who grow into leadership here?"

---

### Round 6: Founder

**Their lens:** Do you believe in what we're building? Will you represent us well with customers?

**What to prepare:**
- A genuine point of view on why Pigment exists and why it matters now. Not a PR summary — your actual take.
- Example: "Planning has been broken at most companies for decades. The tools created incentives to optimize the plan rather than reality. Pigment is interesting to me because it's designed for the world where the plan has to change every quarter — the scenario-first model is a fundamentally different assumption about how business works."
- A question about the future of the product or the market that shows you've thought beyond the job description.

**What not to do:** Do not list Pigment's marketing bullets back at them. Do not say "I love your mission" without substance behind it. Do not ask about comp or territory at this round.

**Closing move:** "Is there anything about my background we haven't had a chance to discuss that I can address before we close?" Then: "I'm genuinely excited about what Pigment is building and I'd love to understand what the next step looks like from here."

## 8. Competitor Matrix

Know this cold. Pigment wins by being faster, more flexible, and more collaborative than legacy players.

---

### Anaplan

**What they do well:** Brand recognition, large enterprise install base, sophisticated modeling capabilities, strong partner ecosystem.

**Where they fall short:** Implementation complexity routinely runs 9–18 months. Requires heavy consultant dependency. Model changes are rigid once built. License structure is complex and expensive. Customer satisfaction scores have declined post-acquisition.

**How Pigment wins:** Time-to-value. Pigment customers go live in weeks, not quarters. The no-code model builder means Finance teams can own changes without re-engaging a consulting firm. For prospects coming out of a failed Anaplan implementation, position Pigment as the antidote to complexity-as-a-moat.

**Key proof point:** Pigment's NPS and G2 ratings consistently outperform Anaplan. Customers cite speed and usability as top differentiators.

---

### Workday Adaptive Planning

**What they do well:** Native integration with Workday HCM and Financials, strong brand in HR-heavy planning, familiar to companies already on the Workday platform.

**Where they fall short:** Competitive only if the prospect is already on Workday. Standalone evaluation rarely favors Adaptive. Limited scenario modeling depth compared to Pigment. UI is dated.

**How Pigment wins:** Flexibility and AI-native design. If they're not on Workday, Adaptive has no home-field advantage. Even Workday shops that want real scenario modeling capability end up looking at Pigment for depth. Pigment's integrations cover the same data sources and are built for multi-system environments.

---

### OneStream

**What they do well:** Strong in financial consolidation and close management, sophisticated for large, complex finance teams, solid CPM capabilities.

**Where they fall short:** Finance-first, not business-wide. OneStream is built for the accounting close, not for the collaborative planning motion that spans Finance, HR, Sales, and Ops. Implementation is heavy. Less intuitive for non-Finance users.

**How Pigment wins:** Collaboration across the business. Pigment is built for the full planning team — not just the Controller's office. If the prospect wants Finance and Sales to plan in the same tool in real time, OneStream can't compete.

---

### SAP (BPC / SAC)

**What they do well:** Deep integration with SAP ERP, large enterprise credibility, broad capability suite.

**Where they fall short:** SAP Planning tools are notoriously complex, slow to implement, and require specialized SAP consultants. Non-SAP shops have no reason to consider them. Even SAP shops often run parallel planning tools because SAP planning UX is poor.

**How Pigment wins:** Speed and usability. For SAP ERP customers who are tired of the planning UX, Pigment is the modern alternative that integrates without requiring a SAP consultant. Position as the planning layer on top of SAP, not a replacement.

---

### Oracle (EPBCS / Fusion Planning)

**What they do well:** Enterprise credibility, integration with Oracle ERP, broad functional coverage.

**Where they fall short:** Similar to SAP — complex, slow, consultant-heavy. Oracle's planning tools require significant IT involvement. User adoption is chronically low. Flexibility is limited without Oracle expertise.

**How Pigment wins:** Business-user ownership. Pigment is built for Finance teams to own their models without IT involvement. Against Oracle, position Pigment as what Finance actually wants to use, versus what IT can manage.

## 9. The Closing Move

Close every round. This is non-negotiable.

**Standard close for every round:**
"Before we wrap up, is there anything about my background we haven't had a chance to cover that I can address?" — pause for answer — "I'm genuinely interested in this role and in what Pigment is building. What does the next step look like from here?"

**If they hedge on next steps:** "What would make you most confident about moving forward?" — this surfaces any remaining objection you can address in the conversation.

**What not to say:** "Let me know if you need anything else." "I look forward to hearing from you." Both are passive. You are an enterprise AE — close every conversation.

**After every round:** Send a same-day follow-up email with one specific insight from the conversation and one sentence reaffirming your interest. Do not recap everything — pick the most memorable moment and reflect it back.

## 10. Red Flags to Avoid

These will hurt your candidacy. Specific phrasing to eliminate.

**Do not say:**
- "I'm a quick learner" — everyone says this. Show it with a story.
- "I'm very passionate about FP&A" — you haven't sold it. This reads as desperate.
- "I'm a hunter AND a farmer" — cliche. Use specific evidence instead.
- "I've always wanted to work in planning software" — implausible given your background. Don't say it.
- "My only weakness is I work too hard" — disqualifying.
- "I closed that deal because I really worked the relationship" — too vague. Name the actions.
- "President's Club wasn't something my company focused on" — sounds defensive. Own the attainment numbers instead.

**Do not do:**
- Ask about comp or territory before Round 5 or 6.
- Criticize prior employers — especially Rapid7 (short tenure, do not volunteer context).
- Over-explain Rapid7 if it comes up. "It was a five-month stint, the market fit wasn't right for me, and I moved quickly to a better fit." Full stop.
- Apologize for gaps. Bridges are not apologies. Frame forward.
- Run a feature demo in the case study. You are not a presales engineer.
- Let the interviewer off the hook at the end of the round without closing for next steps.

**Watch the clock:**
- Recruiter screen: be crisp. They have 12 conversations today. Respect their time.
- HM: go deep on one or two stories rather than covering six stories shallowly.
- Case study: do not fill silence. Silence in discovery means the buyer is thinking. Let them think.
