## 3. PRODUCT OVERVIEW — What Pigment Actually Does

**Core Definition:** Pigment is an AI-powered business planning and performance management platform that connects people, data, and processes in a single, unified system.

### Primary Use Cases
- **Finance / FP&A:** Budgeting, forecasting, scenario modeling, financial close support
- **HR Planning:** Headcount planning, workforce cost modeling, org scenario analysis
- **Supply Chain:** Capacity planning, inventory modeling, logistics forecasting
- **Revenue / Sales:** Sales capacity planning, pipeline-to-revenue modeling, quota setting, territory design

### How Value Is Delivered
1. **Import data from any source** — connects to existing data stack (ERP, CRM, HRIS, etc.)
2. **Build connected plans** — Finance, HR, Revenue, and Supply Chain plans live in one model, not siloed spreadsheets
3. **Real-time scenario modeling** — run "what if" analyses across the whole business in real time
4. **Interactive reports** — executives get self-serve dashboards without waiting for analyst pulls
5. **Cross-functional collaboration** — every team sees the same numbers; version control, commentary, and approvals built in

### Key Product Differentiators
- **Multidimensional planning:** Models can hold complex business hierarchies without breaking
- **Speed:** Build a functional model significantly faster than Anaplan or traditional EPM tools
- **No consultant army:** Designed so internal teams can build and maintain without heavy SI dependency
- **AI-native features:** AI is integrated into modeling, anomaly detection, and scenario generation — not a feature bolted on post-launch

**Saul's bridge (no FP&A background):** You have sold business transformation, not just software. Every CFO you have engaged has cared about planning cycles, forecast accuracy, and scenario visibility. The product complexity is the implementation team's job — your job is to connect business pain to business outcome.

## 4. THE MARKET OPPORTUNITY — Why Now

### The EPM / FP&A Market
- Enterprise Performance Management is a multi-billion dollar market dominated by legacy tools (SAP, Oracle, IBM) and first-generation cloud tools (Anaplan, Workday Adaptive)
- Most companies still run critical planning processes on spreadsheets or fragile Excel-based models — even large enterprises
- The pain is real: slow planning cycles, version control chaos, siloed data, inability to model scenarios in real time

### Why the Timing is the Inflection Point
1. **AI disruption:** Legacy tools were not built for AI-native workflows. Adding AI on top of 15-year-old architecture is different from building AI into the data model from day one. This is Pigment's core structural advantage.
2. **Post-pandemic planning complexity:** Companies learned in 2020 that their planning tools could not handle rapid scenario changes. Demand for modern FP&A tools has accelerated since.
3. **CFO as a strategic seat:** The modern CFO is expected to be a business partner, not just a controller. That requires better tools for forward-looking analysis, not just backward-looking reporting.
4. **Consolidation pressure:** Boards and CFOs are under pressure to consolidate the planning tool stack. One vendor for Finance + HR + Revenue + Supply Chain is a compelling cost and complexity argument.
5. **Anaplan's PE acquisition hangover:** Thoma Bravo's $10B acquisition of Anaplan in 2022 created customer anxiety about roadmap, price increases, and innovation velocity. Pigment has explicitly benefited from this displacement.

**The ARR ambition ($50M → $1B):** This is not a modest target. It signals Pigment believes the EPM market is winner-take-most, and they intend to be that winner — the way Salesforce took CRM or Workday took HCM.

## 6. PIGMENT'S DIFFERENTIATORS — The Genuinely Defensible Claims

### 1. AI-Native Architecture (Not Retrofitted)
Pigment was founded in 2019 — after the modern AI wave began to shift from theory to application. The product was designed to incorporate AI at the data model level, not as a feature bolt-on. Competitors like Anaplan and Workday are adding "AI features" to architectures built before transformer models existed. This is a real structural difference, not marketing language.
**Use when:** Asked about AI, product roadmap, or why Pigment vs. incumbents.

### 2. Speed-to-Value
Implementation timelines are a known pain point in EPM. Anaplan implementations routinely run 12–18 months and require specialized model builders. Pigment's design philosophy prioritizes getting customers live faster with internal teams doing the build — not requiring an army of SIs.
**Use when:** Asked about differentiation, competitive displacement stories, or customer success.

### 3. Unified Multi-Departmental Platform
Most EPM tools were designed for Finance. Pigment's platform handles Finance, HR, Supply Chain, and Revenue on a single connected data model. This is structurally significant — a headcount plan that automatically flows into the revenue model, which flows into the cost structure, all in real time. No stitching together four tools with spreadsheets in the middle.
**Use when:** Asked about use cases, expansion potential, or multi-stakeholder deals.

### 4. Intuitive UX — Business Users Can Own It
Pigment does not require a dedicated technical team to build and maintain models. Business analysts and FP&A teams can build, modify, and own their models. This is the "Tableau moment" for EPM — democratizing access without sacrificing depth.
**Use when:** Discussing champion profile, change management, or competitive displacement.

### 5. Gartner Visionary Recognition (2024)
