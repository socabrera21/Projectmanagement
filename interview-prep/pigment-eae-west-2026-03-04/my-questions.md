# My Questions to Ask — Pigment Enterprise AE West
**Saul Cabrera | Interview Prep | 2026-03-04**

---

## Round 1: TAM Screen (Recruiter)

**1. How is the West territory currently structured — is it split by geography, vertical, or company size, and does this AE role inherit an existing book or is it largely a greenfield build?**

*Signal: Shows you understand territory mechanics from day one, not just that you want a job. Forces the recruiter to give you real intel on ramp difficulty and pipeline health before you commit further.*

**2. What does the pipeline look like for this territory right now — is there deal momentum Saul would step into, or is this primarily a hunting role?**

*Signal: Separates you from candidates who assume deals will be handed to them. Signals commercial maturity and a realistic view of ramp. Also surfaces whether the role is backfill (someone left with pipeline) or net new.*

**3. Pigment has a six-round interview process. I respect that rigor — it tells me you're deliberate about who you hire. Can you walk me through what each round is specifically designed to assess, and where candidates most commonly stumble?**

*Signal: Shows you've done homework on the process, you take it seriously, and you're strategically preparing — not just going through motions. Gives you actual intelligence to differentiate in later rounds.*

**4. The West Coast EPM market is competitive — Anaplan, Workday Adaptive, and OneStream all have established territories here. Is Pigment's West expansion more about displacing incumbents, or are you finding net-new budget creation around AI-native planning?**

*Signal: Demonstrates competitive awareness and market sophistication. Most candidates don't ask about the go-to-market angle this early. It signals you think like a strategist, not just a quota carrier.*

**5. What does the top 10% of your West AE team have in common beyond hitting quota — what behavioral or strategic patterns distinguish them?**

*Signal: Reframes success beyond a number and shows you're optimizing for durability and growth, not just closing. The answer also helps you calibrate your own positioning for later rounds.*

**6. How does Pigment think about the transition from EPM platform to full enterprise data platform — is that a current sales motion, or more of a 2–3 year vision that sales teams are expected to plant seeds for today?**

*Signal: References Pigment's stated product vision without being generic. Signals you understand their strategic direction and you're thinking about how the role evolves, not just what it is today.*

## Round 2: Hiring Manager

**1. When you look at deals Pigment has lost in the West over the last 12 months, what's the most common reason — and how has the team adjusted the discovery or qualification process as a result?**

*Signal: Win/loss analysis is a high-IQ question that signals commercial rigor. Most candidates ask how to win. You're asking how the team learns from failure, which is more revealing and harder to deflect with a polished answer.*

**2. How does the AE-SC partnership work at Pigment — is the SC assigned at a territory level, shared across a pod, or brought in deal by deal? And at what stage does SC typically enter a deal?**

*Signal: SC partnership structure is a make-or-break factor in complex EPM deals with significant technical evaluation cycles. Asking early signals you've run enterprise deals with real SE complexity and you understand the role of technical validation in buying decisions.*

**3. Stephen Macfarlane came from nearly a decade at MongoDB, where the sales motion was highly technical and land-and-expand was everything. How much has that philosophy shaped how Pigment thinks about initial deal size vs. expansion potential in the West?**

*Signal: Name-dropping the Global Head of Sales by tenure and previous company is a signal of serious research. It invites the HM to reveal how sales philosophy is actually operationalized day-to-day, and it flatters Pigment's leadership lineage.*

**4. What does your ramp model look like for West AEs — is there a defined ramp period, and how is quota structured in months 1–6 versus 7–12?**

*Signal: Ramp terms are a legitimate commercial question that top AEs always ask. It signals you're evaluating the role as a business decision, not just asking if you're hired. It also lets you assess whether Pigment's ramp is realistic given the deal complexity.*

**5. What are the deals you've seen AEs lose that they should have won — where did the breakdown happen, and what would you have coached differently?**

*Signal: This is a coaching question disguised as a competitive question. It invites the HM to reveal their sales philosophy, what they value in an AE, and where they believe deals are won or lost. The answer tells you a lot about whether you'll thrive under this manager.*

**6. Pigment was named a Gartner Visionary in 2024. In your experience, how does being a Visionary versus a Leader affect the sales conversation with CFOs who are risk-averse and want to buy from the "safe" choice?**

*Signal: This is a sophisticated market positioning question that signals you've thought about how analysts influence enterprise buying decisions. It invites a real conversation about competitive positioning rather than a scripted pitch.*

## Round 3: Theater Leader (Regional Head of Sales)

**1. What is the one thing you are specifically building in the West that didn't exist 18 months ago — and what does success look like for that initiative in the next year?**

*Signal: Forces the Theater Leader to speak candidly about what the West actually is right now versus what it's becoming. Reveals whether this is a mature territory or a genuine build, and whether there's leadership clarity about the path.*

**2. How do you personally coach AEs on navigating multi-stakeholder EPM deals — specifically when the CFO is the economic buyer but FP&A is the daily champion and IT has a veto?**

*Signal: EPM deals are notoriously multi-threaded. Asking how the Theater Leader personally coaches this dynamic signals enterprise deal experience and invites a coaching conversation rather than a standard interview exchange. It also reveals their coaching philosophy.*

**3. Jay Peir came from Tableau as E-VP of Strategy. In a company that's evolving from EPM into a broader data platform, how does the West sales team stay ahead of that vision rather than being surprised by product pivots that change the ICP mid-year?**

*Signal: Name-drops a senior leader by context and connects it to a real AE concern — ICP and messaging drift. Shows you understand how strategy informs day-to-day selling and that you've thought about organizational alignment.*

**4. What does cross-functional collaboration actually look like between AE, SC, CS, and Marketing in the West — is it tight and coordinated, or do AEs have to drive that alignment themselves?**

*Signal: The real answer to this question reveals how much overhead you'll carry that isn't quota-generating. High-performing AEs know that poor cross-functional alignment is a tax on revenue. Asking it signals maturity and helps you assess role fit.*

**5. Pigment has customers like Snowflake and Siemens — both technically sophisticated buyers who could have built this themselves. What did the sales team do in those deals that convinced them to buy rather than build?**

*Signal: Buy vs. build is one of the hardest objections in enterprise software. Referencing specific named customers while asking the question signals research and invites a real war story, which is the most revealing kind of answer.*

**6. What is your honest assessment of where Pigment's brand awareness is in the West among CFOs and FP&A leaders, compared to the East Coast — and how does that affect how you equip AEs to open doors?**

*Signal: Geographic brand awareness is a real variable in West territory performance that most candidates never ask about. It signals strategic thinking and invites an honest conversation about prospecting difficulty and support resources.*

## Round 4: Case Study (Hiring Manager, Peer, Cross-Functional)

**1. Before I go into my presentation — can you tell me what the one thing is that would make this case study a clear win in your eyes versus a pass? What does exceptional look like here?**

*Signal: Asking this before you present is a power move. It signals confidence, not insecurity. Top closers always clarify decision criteria before pitching. It also subtly resets the panel's attention and signals you run discovery before demos.*

**2. In real Pigment deals, how early does the SC typically enter — and in this case study scenario, at what point would I have brought in a solution consultant, and how would that change the discovery approach?**

*Signal: This shows you think about the full deal architecture, not just your individual contribution. It also invites the panel to validate your deal instincts and signals you respect the SC's role in complex deals.*

**3. The case study gave me a scenario, but in a real enterprise FP&A deal, I'd want to understand the current state pain versus the aspiration gap. Was there a deliberate choice to omit that, and how do you see AEs handling ambiguity in real discovery calls?**

*Signal: This question reframes the case study as a discussion about methodology, not just execution. It shows you can think at a meta level, which is what senior AEs do — they don't just run the play, they understand why the play exists.*

**4. When you evaluate whether an AE is a strong qualifier versus just a strong closer, what does the difference actually look like in a deal review or case study? What are the signals you look for?**

*Signal: Invites the panel to reveal their evaluation rubric, which you can then use to reinforce your performance in the debrief. It also signals that you know qualification is where most deals are won or lost, not at the close.*

**5. After today, what would be the fastest way for me to demonstrate in Round 5 that I've internalized your feedback from this case study?**

*Signal: Closing the round by asking about Round 5 preparation signals confidence you're moving forward, intent to iterate, and a mindset of continuous improvement. It also forces the panel to give you constructive feedback while you're still in the room.*

## Round 5: Global Leader

**1. Stephen Macfarlane — you spent nearly a decade at MongoDB during one of the most transformative periods in enterprise data. What did you see there about how a category gets created that you're explicitly trying to replicate at Pigment, and where do you think the playbook needs to be different?**

*Signal: This is a deeply researched, senior-level question that treats the Global Head of Sales as a peer thinker rather than an authority figure. It signals you've done the homework and invites him to speak about strategy rather than just assess you.*

**2. Pigment's stated vision is to move beyond EPM into the full enterprise data layer. From a sales motion perspective, how does that vision change the AE's job — are we selling to the same buyer, or does that platform vision require us to open doors to a new economic buyer?**

*Signal: This connects product vision to sales execution in a way that signals commercial intelligence. Most AEs don't think about how product roadmap changes the buyer map. This question signals you will.*

**3. How does Pigment currently think about the path from $50M to $500M in ARR from a territory and coverage model perspective — and where does the West fit into that trajectory?**

*Signal: Asking about the growth model at scale signals long-term thinking and commitment. It also gives you real intelligence about how important the West is in the overall plan and whether there's investment behind it.*

**4. When AEs work deals that push Pigment's product capabilities or reveal gaps in the platform, what is the mechanism for that feedback to reach product? And how responsive is Pigment's product team to field intelligence?**

*Signal: This question reveals whether Pigment has a feedback loop between sales and product, which is critical for AEs navigating competitive deals. It also signals that you see yourself as a market intelligence asset, not just a quota carrier.*

**5. What does a top-performing AE at Pigment look like in year three — what have they built, what deals have they run, and what does their career look like at that point?**

*Signal: Asking about three-year trajectory signals long-term commitment and career ambition. It also forces the Global Leader to articulate what excellence looks like over time, which tells you whether Pigment has a real development culture or a revolving door.*

**6. As Pigment scales globally, how do you protect the sales culture that has driven success so far — and what is the biggest cultural risk you're managing as the team grows from 500 to 1,000?**

*Signal: This is a leadership question, not a job candidate question. It treats the Global Leader as a peer leader and signals that you think about organizational culture as a performance variable, not just a perks conversation.*

## Round 6: Founder (Eléonore Crespo or Romain Niccoli)

**1. Eléonore — you came from Index Ventures and Google before co-founding Pigment. Most enterprise software is founded by engineers. What did coming from the investment and business side give you that shaped how Pigment was designed, and is there something customers consistently discover about the product that you didn't expect them to love?**

*Signal: This is a founder question that is specific to Eléonore's background, not generic. It invites her to reflect on what makes Pigment unusual from the inside — and the answer will tell you something real about product differentiation that you can use in the field.*

**2. Romain — you co-founded Criteo, which became a multi-billion dollar public company. What is the single biggest mistake you made in scaling Criteo's go-to-market that you are deliberately not repeating at Pigment?**

*Signal: Asking a co-founder about their previous mistakes is high-risk and high-reward. It signals intellectual courage and genuine curiosity. Most candidates ask about vision. You're asking about hard-won learning, which is more revealing and more memorable.*

**3. What do you wish enterprise CFOs understood about Pigment that they consistently misunderstand or underestimate — and why does that misperception exist?**

*Signal: This invites the Founder to articulate the core value proposition from a position of frustration and passion, not a polished pitch. The unfiltered answer gives you real ammunition for competitive positioning and objection handling in the field.*

**4. Pigment's values include "Be Real, Be Humble" and "Champion Our Customer." When those two values are in tension — when being real about a product limitation might mean losing a deal — what does Pigment's culture actually expect AEs to do?**

*Signal: Values questions are usually soft. This one puts two specific Pigment values in conflict and asks the Founder to resolve the tension. It signals that you've internalized the culture and you're asking a real question, not a flattering one.*

**5. What kind of person ultimately does not thrive at Pigment — not the obvious answers, but the subtle cultural mismatches that you've only learned over time?**

*Signal: This is the inverse of "what does success look like?" and it's far more valuable. The Founder's honest answer tells you more about fit than any positive question can. It also signals self-awareness — you're asking because you want to genuinely evaluate the fit, not just impress.*

**6. What is Pigment's biggest existential risk right now — not the competition, not the market, but the internal risk that keeps you up at night?**

*Signal: This is the boldest question on the list. It signals that you see yourself as a partner in Pigment's success, not just an employee. Very few AE candidates ask a Founder about internal risk. If the Founder answers honestly, you've created a real relationship in the room. If they deflect, that's also valuable information.*
